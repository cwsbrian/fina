# Fina - Pre-Launch Marketing Strategy

**Date:** 2026-05-18  
**Status:** Strategy documented  
**Owner:** Brian  
**Scope:** Pre-launch audience building, social warm-up, and early community formation for Fina

---

## 1. Goal

The immediate goal is not broad user acquisition yet. The current goal is to
build an **early circle** of people who are interested in Fina, understand the
problem, and can get excited as the product is built.

Success means collecting people who:

- Recognize the pain Fina is solving.
- Engage with posts, comments, or DMs.
- Are willing to join a waitlist or early list.
- May become beta users, interview candidates, or early supporters.

This strategy treats social media as a place to build interest, language, and
trust before launch, not as a direct sales channel.

---

## 2. Product Positioning

Fina should not be positioned as another budgeting app.

Core positioning:

> Fina is an AI money companion that understands your spending and helps you
> decide what to do next.

Internal product language can continue to use "AI personal CFO" and
"chat-first financial advisor" as strategic concepts. Public marketing should
be more careful with regulated-sounding terms such as "financial advisor" or
"financial planner," especially in Canada and the US.

Preferred public language:

- AI money companion
- AI personal CFO
- Spending copilot
- Money coach
- Chat-first personal finance app

Avoid overclaiming:

- Do not imply licensed investment, tax, legal, or securities advice.
- Do not promise guaranteed savings or financial outcomes.
- Do not market Fina as replacing a professional advisor.

## 3. Initial Audience

### Primary audience: 20-30s professionals with money anxiety

This is the broadest starting audience. Their pain is emotional and recurring:

- "My income is okay, but I still feel financially anxious."
- "I install budgeting apps and stop using them."
- "I do not just want charts; I want to know whether I am okay this month."

This audience is best reached through relatable posts, questions, and short
observations about the failure modes of budgeting apps.

### Secondary audience: couples and shared household finances

This audience has a sharper pain:

- Money conversations become tense.
- Both people look at the same financial reality differently.
- Shared context matters more than another chart.

This audience fits Fina's future workspace/sharing direction, but should not
dominate messaging until product support is strong enough.

### Feedback audience: personal finance power users

This includes people who have used products like YNAB, Monarch, Copilot Money,
or Mint alternatives. They may not represent the broadest market, but they can
provide high-quality feedback.

This group is best approached through thoughtful comments, product discussions,
and problem framing. Avoid direct promotion in communities with strict
self-promotion rules.

Create two account surfaces:

1. **Founder-led account** - the main posting account.
2. **Brand account** - handle reservation and product identity.

The founder-led account should lead the early strategy because people are more
likely to follow a person building something than a cold product account before
launch.

Recommended founder account:

Recommended brand account:

## 5. Channel Strategy

### Primary channel: Threads

Threads is the best initial channel because the current bottleneck is content
creation, especially short-form video. Threads allows the strategy to start with
writing, observation, questions, replies, and founder-led build updates.

Threads should be used as:

- A content laboratory.
- A place to find language that resonates.
- A way to collect early supporters through replies, DMs, and profile clicks.

Threads should not be treated as the only long-term acquisition channel.

### Secondary cross-posting: X

If the same handle is available, cross-post the best Threads posts to X with
minimal extra effort. Do not build a separate X strategy yet.

### Later repurposing: short-form video

Short-form video should not be the initial production burden. Instead, convert
high-performing Threads posts into text-only vertical videos later.

The production sequence should be:

1. Write a short post.
2. See whether it gets replies, saves, or useful DMs.
3. Turn the best posts into low-effort vertical videos.

### Reddit: research and comments only

Reddit should be used carefully. The goal is to observe pain points and
participate with useful comments, not to promote Fina directly.

Recommended posture:

- Read community rules before posting.
- Comment with useful perspective.
- Avoid dropping links unless explicitly allowed.
- Use Reddit to learn language, not to force distribution.

## 6. Content Strategy

The content should be problem-led, not feature-led.

Main content pillars:

1. **Money anxiety**
   - The emotional uncertainty behind personal finance.
   - "Am I okay this month?"

2. **Budgeting app failure**
   - Why people install and abandon budgeting apps.
   - Tracking is not the same as guidance.

3. **Couples and shared context**
   - Money conflict as a shared-context problem.
   - Same transaction, different emotional interpretation.

4. **AI money companion**
   - Why AI becomes useful only when it understands real spending context.
   - Avoid generic saving tips.

5. **Building Fina**
   - Founder-led updates.
   - Lessons from building a chat-first personal finance app.
   - Product direction without over-explaining implementation details.

Posting ratio:

- 50% problem observations.
- 20% questions and conversation starters.
- 20% build-in-public updates.
- 10% soft calls to action.

Avoid making every post about Fina. The account should become known for
explaining the problem before it asks people to care about the product.

---

## 7. Short-Form Position

Short-form video is not required at the start.

If used later, start with low-friction formats:

1. **Text-only vertical video**
   - Plain background.
   - 4-8 lines of text.
   - No face required.

2. **Screen recording**
   - Figma, notes, mock chat, or product build clips.
   - Use captions instead of complex editing.

3. **Founder voice memo**
   - Voice over a simple screen recording.
   - No talking-head setup required.

Initial allocation:

- 70% Threads/text posts.
- 20% mock chat or screen-recorded concepts.
- 10% voice memo experiments.
- 0% high-edit talking-head videos.

## 8. Warm-Up Plan

Before posting heavily, spend 3-5 days warming up the account.

### Day 1-2: Observe

Search and save relevant posts using terms like:

- budgeting
- money anxiety
- personal finance
- couples finance
- YNAB
- Monarch Money
- Copilot Money
- building in public
- AI app
- personal finance app

Capture:

- Repeated pain points.
- Phrases people use naturally.
- Posts that trigger replies.
- Posts that feel too promotional.
- Questions Fina could answer later.

### Day 3-4: Comment

Leave 5-10 real comments per day. Do not promote Fina yet.

Example comment:

```text
This is exactly why budgeting apps never stuck for me. Tracking is easy to
start, but hard to care about after a week.
```

Example comment:

```text
The emotional side of money is underrated. Most apps show numbers, but do not
help you decide what to do next.
```

Example comment:

```text
Couples finance is so much about shared context. The same transaction can feel
totally different to each person.
```

### Day 5: First post

Start with the problem, not the product.

Example first post:

```text
I do not think people quit budgeting apps because they are lazy.

They quit because tracking expenses does not answer the question they actually
have:

"Am I okay this month?"

I am building around that question.
```

## 9. First 30 Days

### Week 1: Setup and listening

- Create or finalize the founder-led account.
- Reserve the Fina brand account.
- Set up a simple waitlist link.
- Warm up the account through follows, saves, and comments.
- Draft the first 10 posts before posting aggressively.

### Week 2: Problem-led posting

- Post once per day on Threads.
- Comment on 5-10 relevant posts per day.
- Avoid hard selling.
- Track which posts get replies or DMs.

### Week 3: Build-in-public layer

- Add 1-2 posts per week about building Fina.
- Share product thinking without exposing sensitive implementation details.
- Use mock chat examples rather than full product demos.

### Week 4: Early circle call

- Ask for early users directly.
- Invite people who repeatedly engage to join the early list.
- Keep the community lightweight: DMs, email list, or a small private list.
- Do not start a public Discord until there is repeated demand.

## 11. Calls to Action

Use soft CTAs early:

- "Would you use something like this?"
- "If this sounds familiar, I would love to hear your version."
- "I am looking for a few early users to shape this."
- "DM me if budgeting apps never stuck for you."
- "Join the early list if you want to try Fina when it is ready."

Avoid repetitive link-dropping. Keep the waitlist link in the bio and pinned
post.

---

## 12. Success Metrics

First 30-day goals:

- 100 waitlist signups.
- 30 meaningful replies, comments, or DMs.
- 10 user interview candidates.
- 20 people who say they would try the beta.
- 5 early supporters who repeatedly engage.

Leading indicators matter more than follower count:

- People replying with personal details.
- People asking when they can try it.
- People describing the problem in their own words.
- People tagging someone else.
- People asking whether Fina can help with their specific situation.

---

## 13. What Not To Do Yet

- Do not start with high-production short-form video.
- Do not create a public Discord before there is real repeated demand.
- Do not make every post a product pitch.
- Do not over-explain implementation details.
- Do not use regulated-sounding public claims without review.
- Do not promise financial outcomes.
- Do not post aggressively in Reddit communities without reading rules.
